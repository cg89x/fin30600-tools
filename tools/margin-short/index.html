Buying on Margin

§4 · Margin

The deck's SpaceX example: borrow from your broker to buy more shares than your cash allows. Every price move lands on your equity slice — magnified by M = 1/margin — and if the price falls far enough, the broker calls.

Short Sales

§5 · Short Sales

The deck's ATT example: sell borrowed shares hoping to buy them back cheaper. The payoff line flips sign — you gain when the price falls — and the margin call now sits above the current price.

Short selling in practice

  • Stock loan fee — you pay to borrow the shares.
  • Dividends — you owe any dividends the stock pays while you are short.
  • Recall risk — the lender can demand the borrowed shares back at any time.
  • Loan-fee risk — borrowing costs can change while the position is open.
