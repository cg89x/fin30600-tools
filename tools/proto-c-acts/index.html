One idea per act, in the order the lecture builds them: the quote, the book behind it, the depth that can vanish, and the orders that wait.

Act I

The quote is two prices

The deck’s book — best levels only
best bid where you can sell now
↔ the bid–ask spread
best ask where you can buy now

The spread is a round-trip tax — your cost, the liquidity provider’s profit.

Act II

There is no “the price” — walk the book

The limit order book

The arithmetic

Tap an order above — the fills appear level by level, exactly as the book is consumed.

The more you trade, the worse your price — you face a schedule, not a number.

Act III

Liquidity is a quantity

Deck book — 15 / 10 / 10 a side

Thin book — same prices, 5 / 3 / 3

Same order, no depth: a flash crash in miniature.

Act IV

Resting orders wait — and sometimes gap

Price path Your limit / trigger price First bar the order executes

Limit orders fill at the limit price or better. Stop orders only trigger a market order at the crossing price — on a gap (a ±$1 jump in this path), execution can be materially worse than the trigger.

Limit and stop orders trade price risk for execution risk — and a stop is only a trigger.
